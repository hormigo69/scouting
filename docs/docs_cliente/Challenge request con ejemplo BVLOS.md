| Background / Problem

All UAS used in our inspection services portfolio belong to the VLOS (Visual Line Of Sight) category. This technology has a number of limitations, among which the following are highlighted:

Limited Range: Drones operating within the operator's line of sight have a limited range, meaning they can only cover relatively short distances, which can be problematic for inspections in large or difficult to access areas.

Reduced Efficiency: multiple takeoffs and landings may be required to cover an entire area, which can result in reduced efficiency and increased time consumption.

Difficulty in Challenging Terrain: In locations where terrain is rugged or difficult to access, maintaining direct line of sight with the drone may be difficult or even dangerous for operators.

The BVLOS (Beyond Visual Line of Sight) unmanned aircraft systems (UAS) market is growing and experiencing significant technological and regulatory advances. However, it is not fully consolidated and continues to evolve rapidly. 

Advanced technologies are being developed to enable safe and effective BVLOS operations. This includes collision detection and avoidance systems, advanced communications, optimization of autonomous navigation systems, and improvements in aircraft autonomy. 
The market is experiencing increasing competition with the entry of numerous drone manufacturers and technology companies. This is driving innovation and cost reduction. 

Currently, we have contacted Swissdrones, UMS Skeldar, Schiebel, UAVOS and ANAVIA, which are the main players as marketers of long-range UAS within the subcategory of Unmanned Helicopters. Next steps include the signing of NDAs for the submission of use cases.

Functional description of the potential solution

BVLOS UAS can vary in design, size and capability, and are used in a wide range of applications. Here are some of the types of BVLOS UAS that are available:

BVLOS Unmanned helicopters: unmanned aircrafts that operate on the same flight principles as manned helicopters, using spinning rotors to generate lift and thrust. These are the UAVS with the highest capabilities in terms of flight range, MTOW, supported payload, cruise speed, etc. As any helicopter have VTOL (vertical take off and landing) and hovering capabilities.

BVLOS multi-rotor drones: These are the most common and versatile drones that use rotors for propulsion. Although generally associated with short-range flights, some models are designed for BVLOS operations beyond line-of-sight. They are used in applications such as package delivery, precision agriculture and infrastructure inspection.


BVLOS fixed-wing drones: these drones have a similar configuration to a traditional airplanes, with fixed wings that allow them to fly long distances. They are ideal for long-range missions and are used in applications such as border surveillance, pipeline inspection and aerial mapping. This type of drone usually does not have VTOL and hovering capabilities. 

BVLOS hybrids (VTOL): These drones combine features of fixed-wing and multi-rotor drones for versatile performance. They can take off and land vertically as a multirotor and then switch to fixed-wing flight for greater efficiency in long-range flights. This makes them ideal for applications that require a combination of close maneuverability and extended range.

| The factor that most restricts this project is the legislation on BVLOS UAS operations, especially at the European level. 

UAS operations in Europe must pass a risk assessment marked by the 2019/947 regulation. According to this regulation, the operations included in most of our inspection services are categorized under the label of "specific operations", for which the risk assessment is marked by the SORA methodology. Due to the casuistry of the use cases and the dimensional and weight requirements of the intended BVLOS UAS (at least Unmanned Helicopters), the methodology categorizes inspection use cases as medium risk, which forces the implementation of a series of mitigation and safety measures that manufacturers still do not seem to reach yet. 

US and Asia have more lax legal frameworks as far as BVLOS UAS operations are concerned.

Since this challenge is cross business line and end-market we should involve several people with diverse expectations and knowledge base to participate in the whole process. So we could have more than usual difficulties in getting a consensus around the best startups/use cases (see point 5 below).
